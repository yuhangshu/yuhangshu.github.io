About me

Hello

I am a sixth-year PhD candidate in Developmental Psychology at the University of Virginia, working with Dr. Amrisha Vaish in the Early Social Development Lab. Before joining UVA, I completed my B.S. in Psychology at Beijing Normal University and an M.A. in Human Development at Cornell University, where I worked with Dr. Lin Bian on children's stereotypes about intellectual ability.

My research examines children's moral cognition: how they evaluate fairness, respond to inequality between people, decide whom to trust, act prosocially, and explain differences in status and achievement through beliefs about ability and effort. Much of this work is cross-cultural, comparing how children in the United States and China reason about resource disparities and social obligations.

My dissertation, Who is trustworthy? The impact of inequality on children's social trust, asks how children's experiences of unequal treatment shape whom they come to trust — and whether that mistrust generalizes beyond the person responsible for it.

I expect to complete my PhD in Spring 2027 and am currently on the job market, seeking both faculty and postdoctoral positions in developmental psychology.
